Reference preparation:
  prepare_dance46.py uses the pinned upstream CPU converter, velocity capping
  and concatenation recipe. The reconstructed reference is not bit-for-bit the
  original reference; the upstream documentation explicitly describes this
  reconstruction limitation (the source CSVs were resampled to 30 Hz).
  common.py implements the 68-dimensional goal features with NumPy and the
  checkpoint's default pose, avoiding a training-stack installation. Its
  output was compared against the bundled dance22 table: max error 4.77e-7.
  Pinned mjlab robot assets: 8bb2bc9e187797689ec82c78c35019afd2fd0376.

Physical sequence after digital preparation:
  Hoist supports robot; local operator has remote; area clear.
  Start one selected runner and type yes.
  L2+Up -> initial pose / HOLD; allow it to settle.
  Before R1+A: feet flat and carrying most weight, tether just taut.
  R1+A -> stationary policy standby.
  Wait at least 10 s; require R1+X: would be ACCEPTED and stable behavior.
  For motion, tether stays attached with the slack the motion needs.
  R1+X -> selected reference playback.
  After playback, STANDBY_POST keeps balancing. Move hoist overhead, take up
  tether slack without dragging/lifting, then L2+B once supported, Ctrl+C.
  DAMP is latched. Restart the program to leave it. Read refusal/abort reasons.
  Do not raise temperature or motion thresholds to force past a fault.
